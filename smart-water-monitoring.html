Introduction

Water conservation means using water efficiently and avoiding unnecessary loss. A dripping tap, a loose pipe joint or a tap left running can waste treated water. Modern plumbing combines pipes, fittings and valves with efficient fixtures and sensors to make water easier to control and monitor.

This project proposes an inline flow sensor at a tap supply line. A simple display shows daily water use and warns when flow continues unusually long.

Project Summary

Problem identified

Conventional taps give users little feedback on how much water they use. A tap left running or a leak downstream of the sensor can cause water loss.

Proposed solution

Fit an inline flow sensor between a shut-off valve and the tap. Connect the sensor to a microcontroller and a local display or buzzer.

Expected water-saving benefits

Usage feedback can help people shorten unnecessary tap use. An alert can prompt them to close a tap or inspect and repair a leak sooner. Actual savings depend on the site and how users respond.

SDG 6 · Clean Water and Sanitation

The idea supports Target 6.4 on water-use efficiency. Measuring consumption and responding to unusual flow can reduce avoidable freshwater use in homes and on campus.

Learning Outcomes

  • Identify basic plumbing components and common points of water loss.
  • Explain how a flow sensor and microcontroller measure and display water use.
  • Convert flow rate and duration into litres and compare two usage scenarios.
  • Connect practical water conservation to SDG 6.

Conclusion

A smart water usage monitor makes tap consumption visible and flags unusually long flow. It can help users avoid waste by changing habits and checking possible leaks. The project demonstrates how a basic sensor and simple calculations can support more efficient plumbing.
